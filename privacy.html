Grifter Privacy Policy

Last updated November 10, 2026

Grifter is a gift-planning app for small groups such as families. This policy explains what Grifter stores, who can see it, and how to delete it. Grifter is made by an independent developer and isn't affiliated with any company.

What Grifter stores

  • Your account: your email address and the display name you choose. If you sign in with Apple, Grifter gets the email and name Apple shares, which can be a private relay address that hides your real one. If you sign up with email, your password is stored only as a secure hash.
  • Groups: the names of groups you create, which groups you're in, when you joined, and whether you're the group's admin.
  • Messages: what you write in gift-planning threads. Some messages are posted for you automatically, such as when you claim a gift or join with someone's invite.
  • Wish lists: the titles, notes, links and price estimates you add.
  • Claims: which wish list items and ideas you've said you'll get. When you go in on a gift with others, Grifter stores the amount you're putting in and whether you're the one buying it.
  • Gift ideas: gift ideas you jot down for someone else in your group (a title, plus optional notes, a link and a price). They're private to you unless you share one with a group. A shared idea is visible to that group's members, except the person it's about, along with who has said they'll get it.
  • Kids: any group member can add a child (or anyone without a phone) to a group. Grifter then stores the name they enter, the wish list kept for the child, which groups the child is in, and who the child's parents are in Grifter (whoever added them, plus anyone a parent adds). A child profile has no login, email or other contact details.
  • Joint gifts and lists: when someone makes a gift or wish list for several people together (such as a couple, or all the grandkids), Grifter stores who it's for, who made it, and its name if they gave one.
  • Item photos: a photo you add to a wish list item, by choosing one, taking one, or sharing a shop's page to Grifter (which brings the product photo along). Grifter shrinks it and stores it under a random name. Anyone who has its address can open it, so it's shown with the item wherever the item is (your groups, and a kids' share link page for a child's item); the address can't be guessed and the photos can't be listed.
  • Kids' share links: a child's parent can share a link to their kids' wish lists with relatives who don't use Grifter. Grifter stores the link, who made it and which kids are on it. When someone taps "I'll get this" on the page, Grifter stores the name they type and the time. Their browser keeps a secret code so they can undo it; Grifter keeps only a scrambled copy of that code, and nothing else about them (no email or account).
  • Households: if you and other adults make a household, Grifter stores who's in it, household requests and their answers, your household's shared To Do items and checkmarks, and which claims and automatic messages are from the household, so they can read "Everett & Kaley".
  • To Do: the items you add to your To Do list and which entries you've checked off. Your own checkmarks and "just me" items are private to you.
  • Reactions: the reactions (such as ❤️ or 👍) you add to messages.
  • Invites: each invite link is a single-use code. Grifter records who created it and who used it. You send the link yourself, through Messages or any app you choose, so Grifter never sees who you sent it to.
  • Reports and blocks: if you report a message or a person, Grifter stores your reason, any details you add, a copy of the reported message, and who reported whom. If you block someone, Grifter stores that you blocked them. The person you blocked is not told.
  • Notifications: if you allow notifications, Grifter stores your device's push token so it can notify you of new messages. Notifications say only who sent a message, never what it says.
  • Read status: when you last opened each thread, so the app can show which ones have something new.

Like most online services, the servers keep short-term technical logs of requests, such as IP addresses and times, to keep the service running and secure.

Grifter does not collect your location, contacts, photos, phone number, or payment details. It has no ads, no analytics, and no third-party tracking.

Who can see it

  • Your messages, wish list and display name are visible only to people who share a group with you. Your email address isn't shown to anyone, including people in your groups.
  • Every thread is hidden from the person it's about. You can't see the thread about you, its messages, or who has claimed items on your wish list. This is enforced by the database itself, not just by the app's screens.
  • A child's thread is visible to every member of their group, because the child has no account. A child's parents can see who's getting what from the child's wish list.
  • Anyone with a kids' share link can see those kids' first names and wish lists (titles, notes, links and prices) and whether each gift is taken. They don't see who in the family is getting what, any messages, or anything about the groups. The name someone types when taking a gift is shown to the child's groups, like a claim. A parent can remove a claim or turn the link off at any time; turning it off stops the link working straight away.
  • A joint list is edited by the people it's for and seen by everyone else in a group that has all of them. Its discussion is hidden from everyone it's for. A joint gift someone else made for you stays invisible to you until one of its people opens it.
  • Your household's members see your household's shared To Do. Claims from the household show both your names to the group, except on gifts for someone in the household. What anyone plans for one of you stays hidden from that person, even within the household.
  • Reactions are visible to whoever can see the message.
  • Gift ideas shared with a group are never shown to the person they're about. If you leave a group, ideas you shared there stay, shown as from "a former member" without your name, and the group's admin can delete them. Deleting your account deletes them.
  • If you block someone, you stop seeing their messages, wish list, shared ideas and reactions, and household requests from them.
  • Links in messages. Grifter fetches a preview of a link (its title and picture) only when you tap Show Preview. Fetching it, like opening the link, lets that website see your IP address and when you looked.
  • Reports are visible only to the developer, who reviews them to enforce the Terms of Use.

Grifter does not sell your data or share it with advertisers or data brokers.

Service providers

Grifter uses a few companies to run the service. They handle data only to provide that service:

  • Supabase runs Grifter's database and sign-in, on Amazon Web Services in the United States. All the data above is stored there.
  • Apple delivers push notifications, and handles Sign in with Apple if you use it.
  • Resend may be used to email reports to the developer so they get a quick response. That email contains the report.
  • GitHub hosts this website and the pages invite links open.

Deleting your data

  • Delete your account at any time from the account menu on the Groups screen. This permanently deletes your account, your messages, your wish list and its photos, your claims, your gift ideas and claims on them (and anyone's ideas about you), your invites, your To Do items and checkmarks, your reactions, your household membership and requests, and your notification tokens. If you signed in with Apple, Grifter also asks Apple to end its link with your Apple Account. It also deletes any group you created, along with every thread and message in it. Child profiles you added stay: their other parents keep managing them, or the group's admin if they have none. Items on your household's shared To Do stay with the rest of your household. Deleting your account can't be undone.
  • Leave a group from its group details. The thread about you in that group is deleted. Messages you wrote in other people's threads stay until you delete your account.
  • Remove a child from a group (as one of their parents) from the group details. Once a child is in no group at all, their profile, wish list and any gift ideas about them are deleted.
  • Leave a household at any time from the To Do tab. Your household claims show just your name again; shared To Do items and checkmarks stay with whoever remains.
  • Reports are kept after an account is deleted, so the developer can still act on abuse. The deleted account's identity is removed from the report.

To ask a question, request a copy of your data, or have something deleted, email snugger-symptom0r@icloud.com.

Children

Accounts are for people 13 and older. Grifter doesn't knowingly let children under 13 create an account. If you believe a child has, email snugger-symptom0r@icloud.com and the account will be deleted. Child profiles added by a group member (see Kids above) hold only a name and a wish list, entered by the people who added or look after them.

Changes

If Grifter starts collecting something new or uses data in a new way, this policy and its date will be updated before the change takes effect.
